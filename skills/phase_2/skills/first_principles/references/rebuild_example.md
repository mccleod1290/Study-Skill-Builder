# Rebuild: one worked example, with numbers

Read this before the first rebuild (step 5 in `SKILL.md`), and before a figure from the notes goes on a page. Source numbers and marks are in `references/sources.md` and in `SKILL.md`.

The node: a thing costs what it has always cost. The example: materials cost against price. Both cases come from blogs that repeat Musk's own words. The notes did not open the originals [1 L02 g5; 2 M3 g5]. Grades: g5 means the page says it. g3 means a fact about the world that only blogs report here.

## What the example shows, and what it does not

- The method gives a cost floor and a question: where does the rest sit? It does not give the answer. The notes record no outcome here.
- A cost floor is the sum of the parts priced alone. It is a lower bound on cost. It is not the price of the rebuilt thing [2 R5 g4].
- The FS page tells success stories only [1 L06 g5]. The costs it names are effort and, for the why chain, the sense of being slowed and the defensive reaction [1 C08 C14 g5; page rechecked 2026-11-21]. The chef examples Urban lists are all successes [2 L5 g5]. Urban says Musk judged that SpaceX had a reasonable chance of failing [2 L3 g5]. A chef may cook a bad dish [2 L2 g5].

## The routine

Urban's passages, derived (g4) [2 T1].

1. Name the belief "it must be so because it always was".
2. Break the thing into its basic parts. Price each part on its own.
3. Compare the sum with the common figure. The gap shows where the real work sits.
4. Look for the catch. Ask people who know the field.
5. Treat the answer as a hypothesis. Act, collect data, adjust.
6. For a belief of your own, ask why again and again until a stop. Check whether the stop is a fact or an authority.
7. Spend the effort when something new must be decided. Not on routine choices.

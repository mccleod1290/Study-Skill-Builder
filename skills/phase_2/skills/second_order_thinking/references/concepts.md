## Orders

| order | what [1] says | tag |
|---|---|---|
| first | It is fast and easy. It fixes only the immediate problem. Example: hungry, so eat a chocolate bar | [1] g5 |
| second | It is more deliberate. It thinks in terms of interactions and time. Example: picture the habit repeated, then choose better food | [1] g5 |
| third, nth | The page speaks of the second, third and nth order. It gives no separate meaning for the third | [1] g5 |

`rule:` the working definitions of this skill. The second order is what the first result causes. The third order is what the second result causes. The nth order repeats the step. [1] does not define them so.

A chain is not a forecast. It is a list of effects. Each is caused by the one before and has a grade.

## Levels (Marks)

| | first level | second level |
|---|---|---|
| depth | simplistic, superficial | deep, complex, convoluted |
| who can do it | nearly everyone | far fewer people |
| result | a view anyone can reach | a view that differs from the crowd's and is better |
| his example | the company is good, so buy | the company is good, but the crowd rates it too high, so the price is too high: sell |

[2] g5, pp. 2-3, and [3] g5: Marks says it. "Far fewer" rests on [3] only: it says the number of people who can think at the second level is tiny compared with the first level. Marks calls it a bad sign for any attempt at superiority that nearly everyone can think at the first level [2, p. 2].

Edge claim. [1] g5 (says), g3 (true): first-level thinkers all reach the same conclusions, so an edge needs second-order thinking. Marks's own support is narrower, and both parts are about investing: the bad sign above, and his point that a forecast everyone shares is already in the price [2, pp. 9-10], g5. A transfer to security is `reading g2`.

Two ideas, not one. [1] g5 (says): Marks's second level and second-order thinking are the same idea. Marks's second level is about beliefs: what the crowd thinks and what the price already holds. Second-order thinking is about chains of effects. [2] and [3] use neither "second-order" nor "and then what": a search of the text of both found no hit (g4). Keep the two ideas apart. This split is a reading (g4, [1] [2] [3] joined). This skill takes the contrast and the edge claim from Marks, and the chain from [1] and [4].

## Why the stop is a rule

The question has no end of its own. [4] g5: in Hardin's ecolate view time has no end point. Without a stop it can block any change: `reading g2`. So the stop in `SKILL.md` is a rule of this skill, not of the sources.

No source opened gives an objective test for when to stop a chain of whys (g4, [9] and [10] joined). [9] g5: Toyota's article gives none beyond reaching the root cause. [10] g3: Allspaw argues that a root cause is built, not found. The questioner picks where to start and where to stop. A chain of effects has the same problem: `reading g2`. A written reason lets the reader disagree.
